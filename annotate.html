The New England Journal of Medicine 2004-May-20 DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa040938
Activating mutations in the epidermal growth factor receptor underlying responsiveness of non-small-cell lung cancer to gefitinib.
Abstract
Most patients with non-small-cell lung cancer have no response to the tyrosine kinase inhibitor gefitinib, which targets the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR). However, about 10 percent of patients have a rapid and often dramatic clinical response. The molecular mechanisms underlying sensitivity to gefitinib are unknown. We searched for mutations in the EGFR gene in primary tumors from patients with non-small-cell lung cancer who had a response to gefitinib and found somatic mutations in the EGFR kinase domain. All mutations were within or near exons encoding the ATP binding domain of the enzyme, and 8 of 9 were either small in-frame deletions or amino acid substitutions at conserved positions. Two of the most common mutations were seen in other tumors: in 2 of 79 unselected adenocarcinomas and in 1 of 4 large-cell carcinomas of the lung. Kinase activity was increased for the mutant proteins relative to the wild-type protein. These findings suggest that mutations in the EGFR gene play a role in determining gefitinib sensitivity.
